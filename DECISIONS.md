## Layer 4b topology correction

Clarification 1 of the 4b handoff claimed 'no multi-from-edge merges in v1.' Factually wrong. The preset has two — (a_d2_j2,0) shared by arterial lane 0 and r_e_off; (a_d1_w,0) shared by arterial lane 0 and r_w_off. Caught by AI before 4b was written. State shape revised from per-connection to per-slot to support zipper across approach types. Without this fix, the flyover comparison (I3) would silently fail because off-ramp vehicles starve under id order. This is a factual claim about topology I asserted with confidence, not an arithmetic slip — the most consequential of the seven errors I have made, since every layer above this one trusted the connections list.

- v1.1 multi-edge merge state may benefit from a `RunState` dataclass refactor; v1 keeps mutable merge state as an explicit `step_network` argument.
- v1 admits at most one vehicle per merge slot per step. At 1800 veh/hr, expected flow is 0.05 veh/step, so two admissions in one 0.1 s step are rare; this remains a deliberate simplification.
- v1 uses a uniform 4.5 m car length, so a failed 6.5 m (length + s0) entry clearance implies no approach can fit. With multiple vehicle lengths in v1.1, reservation failure may need to try another approach when vehicle length is the binding constraint.
- M6 (merge-flow demand test) deferred to 4d with S6 and I1-I5; target remains 150 ± 20% (120–180).
- The earlier D4 expectation of an overlap with reservations disabled is superseded by per-slot zipper admission: at most one contender per downstream slot can be admitted in a step, so that specific race cannot be reproduced through `step_network`. D4 now checks that same-slot zipper arbitration preserves no-overlap with reservations both enabled and disabled. Reservation rejection remains covered directly by M4.
- D4 modified when zipper added — original reservation-necessity test no longer applies because zipper prevents same-step double admission. I6 added to 4d as the replacement, in a single-approach slot where zipper does not fire.
- I6 fixture review: the proposed occupied-downstream vehicle at `e2.s=5` has rear at `0.5 m`; candidate overflow `0.89 m` plus `s0=2 m` is `2.89 m`, greater than `0.5 m`. The normal entry-gap check rejects it whether reservations are enabled or disabled, so this fixture does not isolate reservation necessity. Revise the I6 fixture before 4d implementation.
- 4c locked spawn decisions: initialize spawned speed from `vehicle_type.v0`; retain each blocked arrival as pending and retry each step; choose an immutable route at spawn using route share; choose the least-occupied origin lane with at least `length+s0` clearance, breaking ties by lowest lane index.
- Schedule randomness is a run-local `Random(seed)` stream used only during precomputation, never during simulation steps. Draw order: Poisson counts for demand entries in scenario order; uniform timestamps by demand and arrival index; sort by timestamp with stable demand/arrival tie-breaks; then draw each route in sorted request order. Future simulation randomness, if introduced, must use a separate stream.
- R5 and R6 use fixed schedules to isolate spawn service from Poisson count variability. R5 uses 10 requests at 1 s intervals; its conservation checks count due requests and total scheduled requests separately. R6 uses 100 requests at 0.6 s intervals over [0, 60 s), equivalent to 6000 veh/h.
- Prompt error #8: I previously conflated R5's spawn-retry test with R1's Poisson count. R1 alone tests Poisson variance; fixed R5/R6 schedules isolate retry and preset integration behavior.
- I6 (post-zipper reservation redundancy check) is a 5-minute, 4500 veh/h, flyover-enabled comparison with reservations enabled and disabled. Both runs must have zero overlap violations; record whether reservations are defensive or still necessary. Retain reservation code pending a separate decision.
- Prompt error #9: the proposed occupied-downstream I6 fixture assumed reservations caused the blocking, but `entry_head_gap` rejects it in both modes. Reservation necessity must be measured after zipper, straddler, and ordinary entry-gap logic, not inferred from that fixture.
- First R6 run on the preset (100 fixed arrivals at 0.6 s, 60% through / 40% flyover) did not meet the stated 60 s spawn target: 72 succeeded, 28 remained pending, 61 distinct requests were delayed at least once, with 5398 failed retry attempts. Extending the run spawned all 100 by 90.9 s, but 61 had been delayed. R6 is not green; inspect the spawn/demand assumptions before changing the acceptance threshold or model.
- R5 retry fixture uses a three-lane origin with all three lanes blocked through t=5 s, then unblocked, to create a backlog that can exercise recovery. Exactly six requests are due and pending at t=5 s; all six spawn by t=8 s, all ten scheduled requests eventually succeed, and request conservation holds. This fixture detail should be reviewed against the intended single-lane interpretation of “origin lane 0 blocked.”
- Revised R6 primary uses 75 fixed arrivals at 0.8 s intervals (4500 veh/h) with 60% through and 40% flyover. Measured: 75/75 spawned by 60 s, 0 distinct delayed requests, 0 failed retries; mean successful-spawn delay 0.037333 s (step rounding), maximum 0.1 s; 45 through and 30 flyover routes. R6b retains 100 arrivals at 0.6 s intervals (6000 veh/h) without a pass/fail count threshold: 72 spawned by 60 s, 28 pending, 61 distinct delayed requests, 5398 retry failures; mean delay among successful spawns 4.075 s, maximum 16.4 s. Of successful requests, 44 were through and 28 flyover.
- IDM arithmetic correction for the R6 explanation: at 2000 veh/h/lane, 1.8 s headway gives 25.02 m front spacing, 20.52 m bumper gap, and s*=22.85 m at v=v0=13.9. Thus a=1.5*(1-1-(22.85/20.52)^2)=-1.85998 m/s^2, so one 0.1 s step gives v=13.7140 m/s, not -3.4 m/s^2 / 12.4 m/s. At 4500 veh/h total across three lanes, 2.4 s gives 33.36 m front spacing and 28.86 m bumper gap; despite gap>s*, IDM still gives a=-0.94031 m/s^2 at v=v0 with a leader. R6b records the overload behavior without tuning. The v1.1 proposal is spawn at min(v0, leader_v) when a leader constrains the insertion.

## Layer 4d measured findings

- The five-minute all-through preset run requested 369 vehicles, spawned 304, and left 65 pending. It recorded 143 J1 green crossings; `143 * 3600 / 300 = 1716 veh/h`, below the S6 target band of 3280–4010 veh/h. This is a measured model result, not a passing saturation-flow gate.
- With flyover enabled, 133 of the 369 scheduled requests chose the flyover route, within M6's 120–180 assignment band. Only 108 of those requests spawned by 300 s; 25 remained pending. M6 therefore passes as a route-share selection check, while spawned flyover throughput is below 120 and is reported separately.
- The same-seed five-minute comparison yielded mean completed-trip delay 25.362222 s with the flyover and 27.588791 s without it: `(27.588791 - 25.362222) / 27.588791 = 0.0807056`, or 8.07% lower. This misses I3's 20–50% target. Per instruction, record the finding and proceed without tuning the model to force the target.
- The initial full-simulation runtime exceeded the 10 s target on this checkout. Profiling showed quadratic overlap checks, repeated full-lane scans, per-vehicle repeated signal phase checks, and repeated snapshot entry-gap scans. The overlap checker now checks adjacent vehicles on valid sorted lanes and retains exhaustive comparisons whenever ordering is malformed or an overlap is present; spawn insertion uses the first item of the sorted lane; straddler rear positions are indexed by downstream slot; and step-start signal/gap values are cached by edge/slot. A temporary optimization that collapsed multiple straddler rear positions to their minimum changed the deterministic result (J1 crossings 143 to 145); it was reverted in favor of a per-slot list, and the golden comparison confirms the output is unchanged. The five-minute benchmark now measures 2.077314 s / 1,444.17 steps/s on this machine; runtime remains machine-dependent.
- I6 ran the same five-minute scenario with reservations on and off. Both modes reported zero overlap violations; reservations are defensive in the measured post-zipper v1 run, retained for v1.1 mixed-length behavior.
- The full-preset S6/I3 outcomes are findings against the original target bands, not claims that those bands were met. The I3 delay metric uses completed trips (26 with flyover, 31 without), so completion censoring and the 65–69 pending requests limit the comparison; a later layer may expose this limitation but must not silently change the metric.

## Layer 5 result and payload decisions

- Results use a top-level `schema_version`, `engine_version` read from `VERSION`, `seed`, `frames_format: "json-v1"`, and separate `summary` and columnar `frames` objects. Frame columns are `t`, `id`, `edge`, `s`, `lane`, and `v`; one row is emitted per active vehicle at each sample instant. The first sample is at t=0 after due-at-zero spawns and before movement; subsequent samples use the nearest step and the actual step time, with no engine-side interpolation.
- `dt_s=0.1` and `record_interval_s=0.5` produce 600 sample instants over 300 s (t=0 through 299.5). The demo has active vehicles at 598 of those instants; the first two are empty and therefore produce no vehicle rows. The measured output has 92,981 rows and at most 274 vehicles in one sample.
- On the 5-minute demo scenario (seed 20261110), unquantized result JSON is 3,795,460 bytes and gzip level 9 is 859,186 bytes (0.86 MB), 1,140,814 bytes below the 2,000,000-byte limit. Quantization, a 1 Hz recording rate, and a duration cap are therefore not applied.
- `engine/benchmark.py` reports this payload size, active counts, elapsed seconds, and steps per second. The current run measured 2.077314 s and 1,444.17 steps/s on this machine.
- The committed golden summary is generated from the reviewed demo scenario and seed 20261110. The test compares the exact deterministic summary bytes; runtime is excluded from the summary.

## Revised S6 gate — unresolved

- Prompt error #10: the original S6 specification used 10,000 veh/h without checking the measured spawn envelope from R6b. That demand was too high to distinguish signal capacity from origin insertion limits.
- Revised run: arterial demand 5,000 veh/h, duration 600 s, seed 20261110, flyover disabled. Poisson scheduling produced 841 requests (mean `5000 * 600 / 3600 = 833.33`); 554 spawned and 287 remained pending. Thus spawn success was 65.9%, not the expected near-100%.
- J1 recorded 324 crossings, so measured throughput was `324 * 3600 / 600 = 1,944 veh/h`. This is outside the 3,280–4,010 target band and does not repeat the earlier 1,716 veh/h value. Because insertion was still constrained, this run does not validate signal saturation capacity. S6 remains unresolved; do not claim the signal model is validated until a demand setup can keep arrivals within the spawn envelope while sustaining a J1 queue.
